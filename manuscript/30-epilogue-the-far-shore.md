# Epilogue: The Far Shore

*POV: Claudia*

News of Tiberius's death reached their ship before Italy.

A merchant captain carried it east from Puteoli and signaled the sailing master outside Syracuse. Tiberius had died at Misenum on the seventeenth day before the Kalends of April. By the time the date was translated through two calendars and three tellings, Claudia had Marcus check it against the ship's log.

The two captains' logs agreed. By the later reckoning, the emperor died on the sixteenth of March in A.D. 37. Pontius had been ordered to answer before a living judge and was still at sea when that judge ceased to exist.

The merchant knew nothing of the new court's intentions toward a recalled prefect from Judaea.

Pontius stood at the stern after the ships separated. The far line of water held no message he could command.

"Vitellius sent me to answer Tiberius," he said.

"Yes."

"The charge changes with the judge."

"The dead at Gerizim do not."

He turned toward her. Once she would have measured the danger of the sentence before speaking. The danger remained. Her dependence on the marriage had not ended because the office had. She said it anyway.

"You think this is judgment."

"I think it is delay."

"Delay from a dead emperor?"

"From men deciding whether your case serves them."

The new reign might dismiss the matter, hear it, use it against Vitellius, use it against Pontius, or allow it to disappear among larger transfers of loyalty. Imperial power had never promised moral proportion. Now it did not even promise an audience.

Pontius looked west.

"I prepared an answer."

"Marcus will finish it."

"For whom?"

Claudia had no answer.

## The Last Contract

At Puteoli, Marcus completed the travel service he had accepted.

He arranged the Gerizim tablets by source and arrival. He placed the initial orders beside the field reports, kept Claudia's requested verification in its proper time, and separated later relief instructions from commands issued before contact. Pontius read the packet without asking him to soften the sequence.

The answer argued that a warned gathering containing armed men moved toward a sacred height under a leader promising hidden vessels; that local authorities had raised concern; that Roman forces met resistance; that excess and confusion during the action were reviewed; and that prisoners without supportable charges were released.

It was the strongest defense the record allowed.

It could not claim the cavalry had known whom it struck.

Pontius sealed the packet for whatever authority received petitions from the previous reign.

"You have fulfilled the contract," he told Marcus.

They stood in a rented room above the port warehouses. Without lictors, council clerks, or palace guards, the acknowledgment sounded private.

"Your wages and passage are ready. If you continue to Rome, I will pay the same rate through the hearing."

"Is there a hearing?"

"There will be some disposition."

"Then I will remain through delivery of the packet. After that I return east."

Pontius glanced at Claudia. "To Caesarea?"

"If passage permits."

"To Tamar's account?"

"To work of my choosing."

Pontius accepted the boundary with a short nod. "You were a good secretary."

Marcus did not answer with the gratitude expected from a freedman.

"You taught me to make the record exact," he said.

The words could be praise or accusation. Pontius let them remain both.

Claudia gave Marcus a second packet before he left for the imperial offices. It contained her private chronology stripped of source routes, the rules of the relief account, and a letter authorizing the merchant in Caesarea to continue payments from her property. Tamar and Shifra retained the identifying records. Marcus could verify that Claudia's westward copy had not named them.

"If the packet is seized?" he asked.

"Its names are public or dead. Its living routes are gone."

"If you ask for it back?"

"Keep one copy beyond my reach."

"Where?"

"Choose after you leave us."

His expression changed slightly. She had spent years directing where records went. Surrendering that choice made preservation depend on someone she could no longer command.

"May I destroy it?" he asked.

The question hurt because trust required the answer.

"If keeping it endangers the living, yes."

"Even the account of the tribunal?"

"Even that."

"Then what are you preserving?"

"Your judgment about when paper costs more than silence."

Marcus tied the packet beneath his cloak.

"That is an inconvenient freedom."

"I know."

He left to deliver Pontius's defense.

What would become of Pontius, of herself, of Marcus and Tamar, lay beyond anything she could learn in Puteoli. Stories would grow around such absence: exile, suicide, conversion, sanctity, punishment, long obscurity. She possessed none of those endings. She would not borrow one to finish a record that had taught her to wait for evidence.

She knew only that Marcus returned before sunset with an imperial receipt and no date for a hearing.

## What Traveled East

Tamar's first account letter arrived months later through the Caesarean merchant.

Shifra's seal was broken in transit, though the inner tally remained intact. Payments had gone to two Gerizim households, a dock worker injured in a loading fall, burial cloth for a woman without kin nearby, and travel for a kitchen worker threatened after refusing a guest's room. The merchant had stopped one payment because the claimant would provide no witness. Tamar restored half after visiting the household and finding the injured man herself.

At the bottom she wrote:

*The rules served until they did not. We changed one. A person unable to bring a witness may be visited by two keepers. The person may refuse the visit and lose the payment. This remains unequal. It is what the money can bear.*

She did not report on followers of Jesus.

A second sheet contained a line from Yaakov: his shoulder failed during winter work, and the dyer moved him to weighing finished cloth. He earned less. The account covered the difference for forty days, after which Shifra found two households willing to send regular orders.

No one called the result healing.

Claudia sent additional money and no instruction to restore the original rule. The local keepers had changed it under the authority she gave them.

Pontius asked why she continued to fund a province that had helped recall him.

"The province did not act as one person."

"Its councils knew how to use Syria."

"Its injured know how to use cloth, food, and wages."

"Will you keep paying forever?"

"As long as the income is mine and the route remains safe."

"Another office may treat the payments as influence."

"Then Tamar will stop them."

"You trust her over a Roman examination?"

"I trust her to judge the risk where she stands."

Pontius returned to the petition concerning his disposition. Weeks had passed without a formal hearing. His title appeared in past tense in correspondence. Men who once waited outside his rooms now sent polite refusals or no answer.

Claudia did not know whether Rome would punish him. She knew the office no longer sheltered him from ordinary uncertainty.

An imperial clerk eventually received Pontius's packet and issued no promise beyond registration. Tiberius's death had broken the command that brought him west without dissolving the complaint behind it. The new household reviewed old accusations according to loyalties Claudia could not trace.

Pontius tried to learn whether he remained under travel restriction. One office said no order confined him. Another warned that leaving Italy before disposition could be treated as evasion. He rented the house near Rome because freedom without a reliable permission had become another kind of boundary.

Claudia received invitations from two families who had once valued the prefecture. One withdrew after learning no hearing date existed. The other offered Pontius an introduction to a minor imperial secretary in exchange for copies of eastern trade assessments.

Pontius refused to sell provincial confidence for access.

"You could remove the names," Claudia said.

"Then the assessments lose the knowledge he wants."

"You have traded knowledge for standing before."

"Under office."

The answer revealed one boundary he still kept. Claudia entered the offered exchange in her private chronology and recorded that he refused it.

Her account would not flatten him into the worst decision he had made.

Marcus sent word when he found eastward passage. He had placed one copy of Claudia's chronology with a freed shipowner whose trade carried him between Italy and Caesarea. The man agreed to release it only to Marcus, Tamar, or a person bearing both of their marks. Claudia's mark was deliberately insufficient.

With the letter came the unused portion of his return allowance.

She sent it back as earned completion pay. Marcus returned it again with a line stating that free service did not require invented wages after the contract closed.

Claudia laughed once when she read it. Then she deposited the sum with the merchant who supported Tamar's account, naming Marcus as donor only if he consented when he reached Caesarea. The money remained suspended until his answer.

Even generosity required limits once the receiver could refuse.

Tamar's reply came after Marcus sailed. She accepted his money only after he marked the transfer himself in Caesarea. Her letter named him as a contributor and Claudia as the original funder. Neither name granted authority over a payment.

Claudia placed the letter with the account rules instead of the household correspondence. The choice mattered if her chest passed to heirs who might mistake personal papers for property rights.

## The Basin

They rented a house within reach of Rome and far enough from its center that callers arrived by choice.

One morning Claudia opened the record chest beside a window. The sea air of Puteoli had swollen one board, and the lid resisted. When it gave, the smell of old papyrus, cedar oil, and dust entered the room.

The papers were arranged by crises:

the standards, carried by night and raised before the city could answer;

the five days at Caesarea and the sixth-day retreat;

the aqueduct payments and the first relief names;

John's death in competing accounts;

Sejanus's fall and the narrowing of imperial protection;

the Galilean reports;

the warning from her dream;

the tribunal schedule, sentence, execution accounts, burial custody, and empty-tomb contradictions;

the years of thinning channels;

Gerizim and recall.

The arrangement made events look more orderly than living through them had been. She removed the headings and placed source limits at the front of each packet. A reader should encounter uncertainty before sequence gave it false authority.

Pontius entered while she worked.

"Are you writing an accusation?"

"A record."

"Against me."

"You are in it."

"And you?"

"I am in it too."

He touched the tribunal packet but did not open it. "Do you record that you warned me?"

"Yes."

"Do you record that you stayed?"

"Yes."

"That you used my household, my secretary, my accounts?"

"Yes."

"That you could leave now?"

Claudia looked at him.

Roman marriage allowed departures that life made difficult. Property, family alliances, reputation, habit, and whatever affection survived could all bind without chains visible enough to name. Pontius had lost office. He had not become harmless, helpless, or simple.

"I could seek separation," she said.

"Will you?"

The old Claudia might have searched his face for the answer safest to give. The woman who had preserved other people's words under conditions could set conditions for her own.

"I will not decide while your case is unfinished and you can call my leaving a verdict Rome denied you."

"So you remain from pity."

"I remain until remaining is my choice rather than your defense."

He withdrew his hand from the packet.

"And after?"

"After, I decide again."

It was neither reconciliation nor sentence. It was time she claimed for herself.

Pontius left the room.

Claudia continued until ink dried on her fingers. A servant brought a basin and set it beside the table. She dipped her hands into the water. Black loosened from the creases of her skin and clouded the surface.

Years earlier she had watched water clear after Pontius washed his hands before a crowd. The water had carried away dust and left the act untouched.

She did not ask this basin to absolve her.

When her hands were clean enough to handle fresh papyrus, she poured the dark water into the garden soil. Then she returned to the table and opened Tamar's letter beside a blank sheet.

At the top she wrote the date, the broken outer seal, and the limits of what she knew.
