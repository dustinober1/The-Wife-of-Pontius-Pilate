# Historical Uncertainty Register

Use these labels in all planning: **documented**, **biblically attested**, **plausible inference**, **disputed interpretation**, **later tradition**, **dramatic compression**, **unsupported and prohibited**. Ancient authors may be partisan or hostile; attestation does not guarantee neutral motive analysis.

| Element | Classification | Control note |
|---|---|---|
| Pontius’s title | documented | Pilate Stone supports **prefect**. “Procurator” for his tenure is prohibited. |
| Appointment c. A.D. 26 | documented / conventional dating | Josephus places succession after Gratus. Exact arrival date is not preserved. |
| Sejanus personally secured appointment | plausible inference / disputed | Possible patronal context, not documented fact. Characters may believe or infer it. |
| Claudia’s existence as Pilate’s wife | biblically attested | Matthew 27:19 attests an unnamed wife and message. |
| Name “Claudia” or “Claudia Procula” | later tradition | Novel may use Claudia as a dramatic choice; do not call it historical attestation. |
| Standards episode | documented in hostile ancient narrative | Josephus supplies night entry, protest, exposed necks, and retreat. Exact year and every military detail are uncertain. |
| Aqueduct episode | documented in hostile ancient narrative | Josephus supplies sacred treasury funds, protest, concealed clubs, deaths. Date and casualty count are unknown. |
| Pilate’s coinage | documented | Lituus/simpulum types are numismatically attested; motive and reception are interpretation. |
| Caiaphas’s office | documented | High priest c. A.D. 18–36. |
| Caiaphas and Annas relationship | documented / biblically attested | Caiaphas is son-in-law; Annas is former high priest and influential elder. Exact division of authority is inferred. |
| Antipas’s rule and marriage controversy | documented / biblically attested | Josephus and Gospel traditions overlap but differ in emphasis. |
| John imprisoned/killed by Antipas | documented and biblically attested | Josephus gives political-preemption motive; Gospels give Herodias/banquet/oath narrative. |
| Date/circumstances of John’s death | disputed | Use a controlled A.D. 30–32 range and preserve competing reports. |
| Tiberias and tomb impurity | documented claim in Josephus / disputed application | Josephus says city was built over graves and settlement incentives were needed. Exact social consequences in scenes are plausible inference. |
| Pilate and Antipas hostility | biblically attested in Luke | Luke 23:12 says they became friends that day, implying prior hostility. Historicity and cause remain disputed. |
| Passover crowd size | disputed interpretation | Large influx is plausible; exact tens or hundreds of thousands are prohibited unless framed as partisan estimate. |
| Roman troop presence at feast | plausible inference | Reinforcement and heightened readiness are likely; exact cohort counts are uncertain. |
| Arrest and Jewish hearings | biblically attested / disputed reconstruction | Gospel sequences differ. Avoid presenting a harmonized chronology as neutral fact. |
| Local versus Roman jurisdiction | disputed interpretation | Roman authority over crucifixion is clear; exact local capital competence and procedure are debated. |
| Tribunal location | disputed | Herod’s palace and Antonia Fortress are major proposals. Novel adopts palace-complex geography as dramatic choice. |
| Lithostrotos/Gabbatha | biblically attested / disputed location | Do not claim archaeological certainty. |
| Herod’s role in Luke | biblically attested / disputed historicity | Adopted narrative element; not independently documented. |
| Barabbas and release custom | biblically attested / historically disputed | Do not describe as a securely documented annual Roman policy. |
| Claudia’s dream/message | biblically attested | Only Matthew 27:19. Content, timing, messenger, and Claudia’s interpretation beyond the verse are invention. |
| Handwashing | biblically attested in Matthew / disputed historicity | May be used as Gospel-shaped public gesture, not routine Roman legal procedure. |
| Crucifixion in A.D. 33 | disputed interpretation / book choice | A.D. 30 is major alternative. Novel commits to A.D. 33. |
| Joseph of Arimathea and burial | biblically attested | Motive, status details, and administrative process vary by Gospel and inference. |
| Empty-tomb reports | biblically attested / disputed historical interpretation | Preserve multiple reports and Claudia’s uncertainty. |
| Gerizim incident | documented in Josephus | Josephus is the principal source and hostile to Pilate; crowd armament and severity should follow his account cautiously. |
| Vitellius’s recall order | documented in Josephus | He sends Pontius to answer before Tiberius. |
| Marcellus | documented in Josephus | Caretaker/administrator; exact formal title uncertain. |
| Caiaphas’s removal | documented in Josephus | Vitellius removes him after Pontius is ordered away. |
| Tiberius dies before Pontius arrives | documented in Josephus | Preserve modest travel chronology; exact arrival progress is unknown. |
| Pontius’s later fate | unsupported beyond contradictory later traditions | Suicide, execution, conversion, exile destination, and martyr traditions must not be presented as reliable fact. |
| Single hidden conspiracy controlling Passion Week | unsupported and prohibited | Catastrophe must emerge from interacting systems and self-interest. |
| Claudia as magistrate, spy chief, or controller of Roman intelligence | unsupported and prohibited | Household-scale access only. |
| Jesus POV or narrated interior certainty | prohibited creative choice | Preserve mystery and external observation. |

## Source posture

Josephus and Philo are indispensable but often hostile to Pontius and write with rhetorical purposes. Tacitus confirms execution under Pontius but supplies little trial detail. Gospel accounts are theological narratives with overlapping and differing sequences. Later apocrypha and ecclesial traditions may inspire reception history but do not establish Claudia’s biography or Pontius’s fate.
