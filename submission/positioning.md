# Positioning and Submission Checklist

## What this book is

A political thriller about institutional failure, told through the one woman positioned to watch it happen and denied every means of stopping it. The religious material is treated as history rather than devotion. The novel takes no position on whether the resurrection occurred; it takes a very firm position on how badly a Roman province handled the days around it.

## What distinguishes it

**Jesus is never a viewpoint and is never explained.** He is seen bound, at forty yards, across a courtyard, and known otherwise through people who disagree about him. This is unusual for the subgenre and is the book's central discipline.

**No conspiracy.** The catastrophe is assembled from jurisdictional ambiguity, festival crowding, a governor who has lost his patron in Rome, a priesthood protecting an institution, a tetrarch declining responsibility, and a dozen officials each behaving rationally. Nobody in the book is running the morning.

**Claudia's power is real and small.** Household accounts, guest lists, messenger schedules, and relief money. She changes outcomes: staff kept, aid moved, a witness protected, a false count corrected, a man freed. She never changes the history, and she is wrong often enough to be trusted.

**Slavery is not scenery.** Marcus is literate, indispensable, and owned. Every use Claudia makes of him is a use of property, the book says so, and buying his freedom is the last thing she does before leaving the province.

## Comparable titles

| Title | Shared ground |
|---|---|
| Hilary Mantel, *Wolf Hall* | Close third on a political operator, procedure as suspense, moral compromise accumulated in small increments |
| Robert Harris, *Imperium* / *Pompeii* | Roman institutional thriller, competence under pressure, documentary texture |
| Colm Tóibín, *The Testament of Mary* | Restraint around sacred material, a woman adjacent to the Passion refusing the received account |

*Wolf Hall* and *Imperium* are the primary comps. Tóibín signals the treatment of the religious material and should be named third.

## Open author decisions

**Section titles.** Each chapter contains titled sections (`## Clubs Beneath Cloaks`). The build renders these as centered scene breaks by default, which is conventional for a submitted novel. `--keep-section-titles` preserves them. Some are genuinely good; others are working labels. Worth one pass to decide, since it changes how the book presents itself.

**Dramatis personae.** Included, but many editors read a cast list in a submission as a sign the book cannot carry its own characters. Recommend omitting unless an agent asks.

**Self-publishing route.** If preferred over querying, the synopsis condenses to cover copy in about 150 words and the positioning section supplies keyword and category material. That is a short job; say the word.
