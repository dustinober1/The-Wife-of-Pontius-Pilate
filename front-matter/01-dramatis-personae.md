# The People of This Book

## The prefect's household

**Claudia.** Wife of Pontius Pilate. Raised as a ward in a Roman house and trained there to be useful.

**Pontius Pilate.** Prefect of Judaea from about A.D. 26. An equestrian officer who believes an even hand and an unbending spine will be enough.

**Marcus.** Enslaved Greek secretary and steward. Keeps the household schedule, the messenger register, and the accounts. Literate, careful, and owned.

**Tamar.** A free local woman employed in the household at Caesarea. Claudia's interlocutor on custom, grief, and everything Roman records do not hold.

**Philotas.** Greek freedman, chief of household staff, inherited from the previous administration. Has served three prefects and intends to serve a fourth.

**Sulpicia.** Senior Roman servant.

**Lucanus.** Roman steward retained from Gratus's household.

**Mattan.** Free local supervisor of market purchasing. Later keeper of the names Claudia will not write down.

**Simon.** A kitchen worker who goes to the Jordan to hear John and comes back changed.

## Rome and the garrison

**Rufus.** Senior officer under Pontius. Loyal, competent, and unwilling to pretend a drawn sword has no purpose.

**Lucius Varro.** Freedman, the governor's legal scribe. Controls charge tablets, custody receipts, and the approach to the judgment seat.

**Sejanus.** Praetorian prefect and, in Pontius's belief, his patron. Consul with Tiberius in A.D. 31 and executed that October.

**Tiberius.** Emperor, absent from Rome, reachable only by letters that take weeks.

**Macro.** Sejanus's successor.

**Vitellius.** Legate of Syria, and the authority to whom every provincial complaint can travel.

**Marcellus.** Sent to receive Judaea when Pontius is recalled.

**Valerius Gratus.** Pontius's predecessor, known to this book only through the files he left.

**Priscus.** A centurion in Germania who signed for goods he had not inspected.

## Jerusalem and the Temple

**Caiaphas.** Serving high priest. An institutional survivor who permits, oversees, and benefits, and puts his name to as little as possible.

**Annas.** Former high priest, father-in-law to Caiaphas, and a power exercised through family rather than office.

**Hanan ben Nahor.** Council elder, authorized to carry the high priest's requests to a new prefect.

**Mattathias.** Junior Temple scribe and liaison. Delivers formal answers and declines to discuss unfinished papers.

**Yoezer.** A gray-bearded steward of a priestly household, and the first person to bring Claudia an account no seal could make official.

**Eleazar.** A younger priest on the cistern commission.

**Joseph of Arimathea.** A man of standing who asks for a body and does not explain why.

## Galilee and the Herodian court

**Herod Antipas.** Tetrarch of Galilee and Perea. Rival, neighbor, and a man whose whole claim rests on Rome believing he keeps his corner quiet.

**Herodias.** His wife, and the most politically intelligent person at his table.

**Salome.** Her daughter, assigned to listen at banquets.

**John.** The preacher at the river. Never seen in these pages, only reported.

**Jesus of Nazareth.** A Galilean teacher, known to this book the way he was known to a Roman household: through distance, rumor, and men who disagree.

## Caesarea and the province

**Rivkah.** Tamar's aunt.

**Yaakov.** Tamar's cousin. Present at the standards, injured at the aqueduct, and unwilling to accept anything from the prefect's hand.

**Miriam.** Yaakov's wife.

**Mara.** A woman who carries accounts between households, and who brings Claudia a warning on her own terms.

**Cleopas.** A traveler on a road, who says he saw something and will not enter a palace to repeat it.

**Barabbas.** A prisoner in Roman custody, accused in connection with violence and killing.
